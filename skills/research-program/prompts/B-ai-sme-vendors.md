# AI Solutions for SMEs (KKV): Bundled Hypersuite vs. Point-Solution Vendors — Landscape Study

## Scope discipline
⚠️ Landscape study only. Deliver a structured vendor map, pricing bands, feature comparison, and industry evidence.
Do NOT produce implementation plans, engineering designs, procurement scripts, migration timelines, effort estimates, or a final "pick vendor X" verdict.

## Context
Baseline: no internal research docs found in workspace; greenfield study.
Goal: a decision-grade map of AI offerings aimed at small/mid-sized businesses (SMEs/KKV), split into (1) hypersuites that bundle AI with their own business software — Google (Workspace + Gemini, Vertex AI), Microsoft (365 Copilot, Dynamics 365 Copilot, Azure OpenAI), Oracle (NetSuite/Fusion AI, OCI), SAP (Joule, Business AI) — and (2) point-solution ("céleszköz") vendors selling standalone AI tools (e.g. HubSpot AI/Breeze, Zoho Zia, Salesforce Einstein/Agentforce for SMB tiers, Freshworks Freddy, Intercom Fin, Notion AI, Jasper, Motion, Fireflies.ai, plus vertical tools for accounting, HR, legal, support).
Key comparison axes: published pricing (per-seat/month, usage-based, SMB tiers), packaging (add-on vs. included), shared features (copilots for email/docs/CRM/meetings, chatbots, summarization, reporting) vs. unique differentiators (agents, vertical depth, data residency, EU/HU language support, integrations, lock-in).

## Primary question
Which AI vendors are actively selling to SMEs in 2024–2026 — hypersuite-bundled vs. point-solution — and how do their pricing models and feature sets compare: what is commoditized, what is differentiated, and what are the trade-offs (TCO, lock-in, integration, vertical fit) between the two classes?

## Sub-questions (cover all)
A) Vendor map: hypersuites (Google, Microsoft, Oracle, SAP — AI packaging on their own ERP/CRM/office stacks) vs. point solutions (HubSpot, Zoho, Freshworks, Intercom, Notion, Jasper, Slack/Zoom AI, vertical specialists); reference packaging models (bundled, add-on seat, usage/token, agentic credits).
B) Pricing evidence: verbatim published SMB prices (e.g. Microsoft 365 Copilot ~$30/user/mo, Gemini for Workspace tiers, Copilot Studio credits, HubSpot Breeze, Zoho Zia bundles, Freshworks Freddy, Intercom Fin per-resolution); hidden costs (minimum seats, data-prep, integrations).
C) Feature matrix: identical table-stakes (meeting notes, email drafting, doc summarization, CRM enrichment, support chatbot) vs. unique capabilities (Salesforce/Agentforce autonomous agents, SAP Joule ERP grounding, Oracle Fusion AI in finance, vertical/accounting/HR/legal point tools); language support incl. Hungarian; EU data residency.
D) Adoption & workflow: deployment effort for non-IT SMEs, admin/permission models, human-in-the-loop defaults, proven SMB adoption patterns and churn drivers.
E) Guardrails & economics: data use/training opt-outs, compliance (GDPR, EU AI Act posture), security certifications, eval/QA of AI output; TCO order-of-magnitude for a 10–100-seat SME; latency/limits where published.
F) Integration & lock-in: how each hypersuite's AI ties to its own stack vs. open point tools; connectors to ERP/CRM/accounting (Xero, QuickBooks, Exact, DATEV); API/export freedom.
G) Named 2024–2026 successes AND failures/rollbacks: Copilot seat-expansion struggles, Gemini Workspace repricing, AI-pricing backlash, SMB pilot failures; where point solutions won/lost vs. bundles.
